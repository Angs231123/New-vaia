About VAIA

Who we are, and how we operate

The Virtual Australian International Airshow (VAIA) is a volunteer virtual special-operations demonstration team on the VATSIM network. This page sets out our mission and the operating framework we fly under.

Our Story

Bringing Avalon's atmosphere into the sim

VAIA was formed by a group of virtual pilots who wanted the excitement of a real international airshow — formation passes, aerobatic solos, tight ATC choreography — recreated properly, and taken seriously, within the flight sim community.

We fly out of a virtual Avalon Airport (YMAV) in Victoria, Australia, the historic home of the real-world Australian International Airshow, and build our display calendar, rehearsals, and pilot standards around that same spirit of showmanship and safety.

Operating Framework

vSOA Classification & Compliance

As a display and demonstration team, VAIA operates under a defined set of policies so that pilots, spectators, and local ATC always know what to expect from us.

① CLASSIFICATION

VAIA is registered and operates as a Special Event / Demonstration Team under the vSOA (Virtual Special Operations Area) framework. This classification covers organised formation, aerobatic, and display flying that falls outside normal day-to-day network traffic, and requires the additional coordination and reporting standards set out below.

② FLIGHT TRACKING & CURRENCY

To remain an active, display-qualified unit, VAIA's 10 required flights every 60 days are flown exclusively by our official rostered pilots, under our designated organisational callsign. This ensures every logged activity reflects a genuine, accountable member of the team rather than casual or unaffiliated traffic.

  • Only pilots listed on the current Performance Roster may log flights under the VAIA callsign.
  • Flights are logged with date, route/display profile, and pilot VATSIM CID for auditing.
  • Currency is reviewed on a rolling 60-day basis by the Airshow Director.

③ AIRSPACE COORDINATION

Because our displays involve aerobatics, tight formations, and unusual flight profiles, VAIA pre-coordinates every practice and public display with the controlling VATSIM ATC facility for the airspace in use (local vACC / ARTCC):

  • Advance notice: Display windows are submitted to the relevant vACC ahead of the event, including block times, altitudes, and the display box/track we intend to fly.
  • Dedicated frequency: Where available, a discrete display/practice frequency is arranged with ATC to keep formation and aerobatic traffic clear of the general flying frequency.
  • Live briefing: A short-notice briefing is given to the controller on duty before each display block, covering formation composition, manoeuvres, and abort/go-around procedures.
  • ATC authority: The controlling ATC facility retains full authority to amend, hold, or cancel any VAIA activity for traffic or safety reasons — our display plan is always subordinate to their instructions.
  • Real-world safety practice: Formation and aerobatic sequences are separated from other simulated traffic using the same deconfliction principles as real-world airshow operations.

④ NETWORK STANDING

VAIA operates openly and transparently on the VATSIM network. We are not a network authority and hold no special privileges beyond what any coordinated event organiser is granted by the controlling facility. Full network rules, as published at vatsim.net, always take precedence over anything on this site.
